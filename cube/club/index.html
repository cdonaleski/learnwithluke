The Idea

A handful of people who like twisty puzzles, meeting to solve them together. Beginners entirely welcome — most of a club like this is somebody leaning over and saying "no, look, do that bit first".

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Who it's for

Anyone who can already solve a cube, and anyone who cannot yet but would like to. Those two groups need each other: the second learns faster with help, and the first learns faster by explaining.

⏱️ What happens

Solve, time each other, compare methods, and work through the awkward cases together. Somebody brings a cube nobody can solve and everyone crowds round it.

📚 What you would need

A 3×3 cube, and that is all. A cheap one is fine to start; the fancy magnetic ones matter only once you are already fast.

🎯 What it is not

Not a competition, not a class, and not something you can be behind on. Come to one, come to none, come back after a year.

The Rules

Short, and mostly common sense. They exist so that the person who is slowest in the room still wants to come back.

  1. Everyone is welcome, at any speed. Nobody is too slow to be here. A two-minute solve and a ten-second solve get the same respect.
  2. Ask before you touch someone's cube. People set their puzzles up how they like them. Pick one up uninvited and you have undone somebody's afternoon.
  3. Help when you are asked, not before. Blurting out the answer while somebody is thinking takes the whole point away.
  4. Bring your own cube. Any cube. It is the practice that makes you fast, not the puzzle.
  5. Scrambles are honest. Times only mean something if everybody is solving the same sort of thing.
  6. Leave it tidy. The room should look better when we go than when we arrived.

Competitions

Real competitions, run by the World Cube Association. Anyone may enter one — you do not have to be fast, and every competitor there was a first-timer once.

Details come from each competition's own page, which is always the truth. You need a free World Cube Association account to enter.

Going to your first one

😌 You are not too slow

There is no minimum speed. People who take two minutes compete alongside people who take ten seconds, and nobody minds.

⏳ Cut-offs are real though

Some events stop you after a set time and move on. It keeps the day running — but read which cut-offs apply before you enter.

🧊 Bring your own cube

You solve on your own puzzle. Bring the one you practise on.

🙋 You will also judge

Competitors help run the day, judging and scrambling between their own rounds. It is the fastest way to meet everybody.

Meet-ups

Competitions are the big days out. Meet-ups are the ordinary ones — a few people, a table, and some cubes.
