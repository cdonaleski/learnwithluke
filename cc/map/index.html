🗺️ States and Capitals
How it works
Find it — on your own
The page names a state and you tap it on the map. A wrong tap tells you which state you did touch; a second wrong tap shows you where the right one is. Nothing here counts toward learned — it is practice.
Name it — with a parent
One state lights up. You point to it and say its capital and its name, the way the proof asks — "Augusta, Maine." Whoever is checking has the answer on screen and marks it. This is what fills in the geography dots on the Year at a Glance, three right in a row, the same as Recite.
The small states
Rhode Island, Delaware and the District of Columbia are a few pixels wide on a whole-country map, so the Northeast is drawn again, close up, beside it. Tap either one.
Weeks 1 to 10
The states and capitals are weeks 1 to 10 of Cycle 3, five a week in the order of the proof sheet, with Washington, DC in week 3.
Santa Fe
The proof sheet prints “Sante Fe, NM”. That is a misprint — Classical Conversations' own map, on the same pages, says Santa Fe, which is the city's name — so Santa Fe is used here.
Where the map comes from
U.S. Census Bureau state boundaries (2017), which are public domain. It is not traced from the map in the Foundations Guide.
