How It Works

What counts as learned
A card counts as learned once you have said it right three times in Recite. Cards and Match are practice and do not count — picking the right answer out of five is easier than saying the whole thing from nothing, and proof day is the second one.
Why the same cards keep coming back
Whatever you missed comes first next time, then the ones you have never seen, and the ones you already know come last. Time spent on what you know is time not spent on what you do not.
Sections, not weeks
The numbers are the gray boxes on the proof sheets, because that is how the proof is given: a tutor says "section seven, thirteens" and you recite the whole box. Tap several to revise a run of them.
Recite — the real one
You see the prompt and nothing else. Say the whole answer out loud, then mark yourself honestly. Pressing "I said it all" when you only got half is the one way to make this useless.
Where history is
The history sentences are Classical Conversations' own writing, out of the Guide they sell, so they are not published here. They live in the family's own copy of this page at home, word for word — which is what proof day needs.
