How It Works

On your own: Cards and Match
Cards shows one prompt, you say it, then turn it over to see if you were right. Match pairs prompts with answers — easier, because picking the right one out of a handful is not the same as producing it from nothing. Start here on new material.
With someone else: Recite
This is the real thing and it takes two people. Hand the device to a parent or a friend: they read the prompt out loud, you say the whole answer back, and they check you against what is on the screen. That is exactly how the proof is given — four times over, to a parent, another parent, your tutor, and the director.
Easy, Medium, Hard
Not harder cards — the same cards with less help. Easy shows the first letter of every word, which is how people have memorized long passages for centuries. Medium gives you the opening and tells you how much is left. Hard gives you nothing, which is what proof day gives you. In Match, the level decides how many pairs are on screen at once; in Recite it is how much the checker is allowed to give you when you stall — the opening words, or just how many words there are.
What counts as learned
Only Recite counts it, three times right in a row, and one miss resets the run. Cards and Match are practice. Pressing "Said it all" when only half came out is the one way to make this useless.
Why the same cards keep coming back
Whatever was missed comes first next time, then the never-seen, and the ones already known come last. Time spent on what you know is time not spent on what you do not.
Sections, not weeks
The numbers are the gray boxes on the proof sheets, because that is how the proof is asked: "section seven, thirteens" and you recite the whole box. Tap several to revise a run of them.
Latin goes both ways
Every Latin card is stored as a pair, so it can be asked in either direction. As taught follows the sheet — words for their meaning, verses in Latin. → Latin is the harder one: you hear the English and have to produce the Latin. Each direction is tracked separately, because they really are different skills.
Where history is
The history sentences are Classical Conversations' own writing, out of the Guide they sell, so they are not published here. They live in the family's own copy of this page at home, word for word — which is what proof day needs.
